# Full SaaS commercialization blueprint

Use this as the output contract for a full blueprint. Adapt depth to the product and evidence. Localize headings to the user's language. Do not preserve an empty section merely to satisfy the template; mark material unknowns explicitly.

For an early product or small team, keep the main plan focused on decisions and move exhaustive inventories to appendices. When blocking evidence is missing, label the document **Decision draft** and include the evidence required for a later **Verified plan**.

## 1. Commercial verdict

- Recommended direction in one paragraph
- Why this product can or cannot support a SaaS model
- Primary customer, paid outcome, business model, and launch motion
- Three decisions that determine the plan
- Go, conditional go, pivot, or no-go verdict with conditions

## 3. Product and market definition

- Current product and maturity
- Core job and measurable customer outcome
- Primary ICP, user, buyer, approver, and beneficiary
- Trigger event and current alternatives
- Differentiation and defensibility
- Positioning statement
- Explicit non-target customers

## 4. Commercial offer

- Recommended revenue model and value metric
- Packaging and entitlement table
- Pricing and billing cadence
- Trial, pilot, or proof mechanism
- Upgrade, downgrade, overage, cancellation, refund, and discount rules
- Price-validation experiment and decision threshold

- Initial target cohort and launch offer

- Activation definition and onboarding path
- Retention, expansion, and referral mechanisms
- First experiments with hypotheses, thresholds, owners, and stop conditions

Include support, incidents, billing exceptions, refunds, abuse, security, privacy/data requests, account recovery, vendor failure, customer feedback, and reconciliation as applicable.

- Formula definitions and source assumptions
- Conservative, base, and upside scenarios
- Revenue, gross margin, contribution margin, CAC, payback, churn/NRR, break-even, and runway as available

| Scenario | Paying customers | ARPA/usage | Revenue | Gross margin | Contribution | Churn | CAC/payback | Cash implication |

If inputs are missing, provide formulas and clearly labeled placeholders or ranges rather than invented precision.

## 10. Metrics and management cadence

- North-star metric
- Funnel and cohort metrics
- Financial metrics
- Reliability, support, security, and abuse guardrails
- Definition, source, owner, cadence, and response threshold for each critical metric

## 11. Legal, compliance, and risk gates

- Applicable policy and contract surface
- Jurisdiction-specific questions requiring professional review
- Data, security, tax, payments, consumer, licensing, export-control, sanctions, or sector gates as applicable
- Risk register with triggers, mitigations, contingencies, and owners

## 12. Release gates and prioritized roadmap

State separate evidence gates for:

- **Private paid pilot:** a bounded cohort can pay and receive the outcome with supervised operations;
- **Public self-serve launch:** unknown customers can buy, use, renew, obtain support, and leave safely;
- **Scale:** acquisition and usage can grow without breaking margin, reliability, support, reconciliation, or compliance.

Organize by outcome, not department:

- **0–30 days:** validate the customer, offer, payment feasibility, and minimum commercial path
- **31–60 days:** implement and operate the smallest paid loop
- **61–90 days:** improve retention, reliability, reconciliation, and repeatable acquisition
- **Post-validation:** only the scale investments justified by named thresholds

If customers already exist, include a reconciled migration and rollback workstream.

| Priority | Outcome | Deliverable | Owner role | Dependency | Acceptance evidence | Exit criterion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

## 13. Immediate decisions and next actions

- Decisions the user can approve now
- Blocking questions and why each matters
- Next seven actions in order
- What not to build or spend on yet

## Final consistency check

End only after confirming:

- the buyer can purchase through a feasible path;
- payment produces the correct entitlement;
- customer value and pricing use the same value metric;
- the operating team can support every paid promise;
- the economics include real variable costs;
- activation and retention are measurable;
- the roadmap has evidence-based exit criteria;
- assumptions, sources, and professional-review needs are visible.
