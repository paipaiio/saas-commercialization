# Go-to-market and retention

Read this reference for launch strategy, acquisition, sales motion, onboarding, lifecycle messaging, retention, expansion, and referral.

## Start from the buying path

Map:

`Trigger → Search/discovery → Evaluation → Proof → Purchase → Activation → Habitual value → Renewal → Expansion/referral`

For each stage define the buyer question, required proof, asset or product experience, owner, event, metric, and next action.

## Channel selection

Score candidate channels on:

- concentration of the primary ICP;
- buyer intent;
- trust transfer;
- time to first evidence;
- cash requirement;
- content or operational burden;
- scalability after initial validation.

Select one primary acquisition channel and at most two supporting channels for the first cycle. Avoid “do SEO, social, ads, partnerships, communities, email, and affiliates” plans with no prioritization.

## Common motions

### Self-serve or product-led

Requires low setup friction, fast time to value, understandable pricing, in-product proof, self-service billing, and support that does not consume the gross margin.

### Sales-assisted

Requires qualification criteria, discovery questions, proof or demo, proposal/quote process, CRM state, handoff to onboarding, and loss reasons.

### Enterprise

Requires a justified contract value and commonly adds security review, legal negotiation, procurement, SSO, audit evidence, invoicing, implementation, and customer success. Do not pursue it only for prestige.

### Usage-led or developer-first

Requires excellent documentation, fast key issuance, observable usage, predictable limits, transparent error behavior, and an upgrade path before hard limits interrupt production.

## Launch offer

Define:

- narrow target cohort;
- offer and price;
- evidence promised;
- onboarding commitment;
- capacity limit;
- feedback cadence;
- success and stop thresholds;
- how pilot terms transition to normal terms.

Do not call unpaid curiosity “demand.” Prefer behavior carrying cost or commitment: payment, deposit, signed pilot, production integration, repeated use, or credible referral.

## Activation and retention

Choose one activation event that represents first realized value, not setup completion. Measure:

- signup-to-activation conversion;
- median and percentile time to value;
- retention by acquisition cohort;
- recurring use of the core value action;
- support burden and failure reasons;
- downgrade, cancellation, and involuntary churn.

For churn, collect a structured reason and distinguish:

- never activated;
- insufficient value;
- missing capability;
- reliability or trust failure;
- price or budget;
- seasonal or temporary pause;
- customer business failure;
- payment failure;
- abuse or policy termination.

## Sales and lifecycle assets

Only recommend assets tied to a stage:

| Stage | Possible asset |
| --- | --- |
| Discovery | Problem-led landing page, comparison, technical article, directory listing |
| Evaluation | Interactive demo, benchmark, security overview, case study, ROI model |
| Proof | Trial, sandbox, paid pilot, sample project, migration assessment |
| Purchase | Pricing page, quote, order form, procurement pack |
| Activation | Checklist, sample data, guided setup, office hours |
| Retention | Usage report, success review, alerts, education, roadmap communication |
| Expansion | Limit warning, additional use case, team invitation, annual plan |

## Experiment design

For each experiment specify:

- hypothesis;
- target segment;
- changed variable;
- primary metric and guardrail;
- minimum evidence or timebox;
- decision rule: continue, change, or stop;
- owner and next action.

Do not optimize conversion before confirming that activated customers retain and produce acceptable contribution margin.
